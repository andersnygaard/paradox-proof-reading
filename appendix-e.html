Appendix E

Sources and method

This book was written from an archive, and the archive is unusual for a boat of fourteen feet. Almost everyone who has built or sailed a Paradox wrote about it, and most of what they wrote survives. This appendix says what the archive holds, where the design has appeared in print, how the book's claims were checked and what rules governed what was left out.

The archive

The builders' mailing list. Glen Maxwell founded the Paradox builders' group on Yahoo Groups on 7 July 2001, for builders to exchange information. A second Yahoo group took the photographs when the first ran out of room in 2004, and when Yahoo announced the end of its groups in 2019 the archive was moved to groups.io, where the list continues as paradoxsailboat. The complete run from July 2001 to 2025 was decoded for this book: 10,065 messages. The messages of the Yahoo years carry numbers, and the chapters cite them by number and date ("message 882, 2003-03-12"); the groups.io messages carry no number and are cited by date and archive page. The list is the book's backbone. It holds the builders' questions and answers as they were asked, Layden's own two messages of 2003, the sea trials, the knockdowns, the sales, the deaths and the eighteen years of Bill Serjeant, Alastair Law, Dave Bolduc and others answering the same questions patiently for each new builder.

The Facebook groups. Alberto Callejon opened a "Paradox" group in 2011. Tim McKinney opened the "Paradox Sailboat" group in February 2019, and most of the day-to-day conversation has happened there since. The 833 posts of the 2019 group, with their comments, were captured for this book through September 2026 and are cited by the post's date and author. The Facebook years are the least mined part of the record, and the chapters say so where it matters.

Builders' own blogs and websites, mirrored in full: Bill Serjeant's Bill's Log (2005 to 2018, 447 posts) and his earlier UK Paradox website (2003 to 2009); Alastair Law's Little Jim pages (2003 to 2023); Pete Martin's JoHanna build log (2006 to 2011); Lezlie Henson's Tardis blog (about 2011 to 2013); Sean Mulligan's Full n By (2013 to 2014); Dave Skaife's cruising log (2013 to 2018, 160 posts); Eerik Mee's THU pages; Geoff Davis's Small Fry pages; Dave Raftery's wiki comparing Paradox with the Enigmas; Matthew Noonan's build log; Gavin Atkin's intheboatshed posts on the UK boats; and the Cruising Ashiki blog, whose crew met Small Fry in Shark Bay in 2014.

The Bolducs' microcruising.com, David and Mindy Bolduc's site (135 pages, about 1993 to 2019), is the closest firsthand source on Layden the book has: "Matt's Boats", the Little Cruiser and Paradox pages, the frequently asked questions, the trip logs, the 2019 Cedar Key report and the news pages. It is also where the plans have been sold since the beginning.

Matt Layden's own words, collected in appendix D: his two list messages of 2003, his seven build-log posts on his brother Andrew's website in 2005, his Sand Flea announcement of 2007 as relayed to the messing-about.com forum, his checkpoint notes from the 2003 Everglades Challenge and his two interviews with Small Craft Advisor (2009 in print, 2022 online).

Sven Yrvind, in his own writing on yrvind.com, in Catalyst no. 32 (2008), in two Furled Sails podcast interviews of July 2007 and in his near-daily workshop videos, of which the two retrospectives of July 2025 on the 1997 meeting and the chine runners, and his video of 14 September 2026 announcing a new boat, were transcribed. He also answered a question for this book by email in 2026, on why a chine-runner boat needs somewhat less stability than a centreboard boat. Swedish press coverage of Yrvind from 2020 to 2026 was read and found to say nothing about Layden or Paradox.

Race records. WaterTribe's own results pages for every Everglades Challenge, Ultimate Florida Challenge and Ultra Marathon from 2001 onward (109 pages), the Race to Alaska's entry pages for 2024 to 2026 and Doug Cameron's blog of the WaterTribe years.

Magazines, journals and newsletters, transcribed page by page: 54 issues of Messing About in Boats from 1991 to 2025, including Don Elliott's sixteen-part "Building Paradox"; the relevant issues of Small Craft Advisor; Catalyst no. 32 and the AYRS journals of 1976; the Dinghy Cruising Association's Bulletin 193; the Amateur Boat Builders' Association newsletter of April/May 2021; Sea People no. 64 (2006); Duckworks Magazine; and SAIL's "Go Small, Go Now" (2014).

Podcasts and video. The Furled Sails interviews with the Bolducs (two parts, July 2006) and with Yrvind (two parts, July 2007); a playlist of 179 videos of Layden-family boats by 33 uploaders, with captions transcribed where they existed; Alastair Law's talk to the Amateur Yacht Research Society in May 2020; Pete Martin's walkthrough of JoHanna; Roger Barnes's film of the Bideford Bar incident; the Sailing Pathfinder build series; and Will Robertson's video of his own plans-free Paradox-type hull, built without plans and sailed in the Texas 200. Where the book quotes from an auto-generated caption track it says so.

Forums. Three threads on boatdesign.net (2011 to 2020, 675 posts), twenty topics on messing-about.com (2004 to 2012), one thread on the Australian Woodwork Forums (2007 to 2009, the controlled winglet retrofit of a Bolger Teal) and an attempted capture of two Sailing Anarchy threads that the site's bot wall defeated.

Documents and plans. The Gorfnik plans of 2015; the Paradox study plans; the first edition of this book (2023 to 2025) and its earlier drafts; and an interview with the author about the Miramis build.

The research wiki. Before this book was written, the whole archive above was read into a public wiki of about 430 pages, one page for every boat, person, component and source, with every statement carrying a numbered citation into a reference page. The book's chapters were researched from the wiki and from the raw sources behind it. The wiki is a map of the archive, distilled and in places second-hand; where it and a raw source disagreed, the raw source won, and the disagreements found while writing are listed below.

The design in print

The Paradox has never been advertised. These are the places where she has appeared in print, in order.

  • 1991 to 1995. Messing About in Boats carries the Bolducs' early accounts of Little Cruiser and their meeting with Layden, and a reader's recollection of Terrapin off Florida and Georgia in 1983 and 1984 ("Layden Sightings", vol. 14 no. 16).
  • 15 July 1995. Messing About in Boats introduces Paradox to print: the Bolducs' "Cheating Winter", a firsthand account of the January 1994 Gulf Stream crossing in company with Layden's boat, with the sail plan dated July 1994 and the specification box.
  • 15 September 1996 to 1 May 1997. Don Elliott's "Building Paradox", sixteen parts, Messing About in Boats vol. 14 nos. 9 to 24, written while he built The Edge. Sold afterwards as a booklet, and still sold by his family.
  • 1997 to 1998. Letters and replies in Messing About in Boats on the series and on epoxy accuracy: the reaction split cleanly, one reader calling the series helpful and another, in the same volume, "the most boring thing ever published"; James Watson, a technical advisor at the epoxy maker Gougeon Brothers, published a detailed correction of the series' epoxy technique and trademark use, while allowing the articles had "much merit."
  • May/June 2000. Small Craft Advisor no. 3, the Bolducs' "About Us" interview, known from its reprint on microcruising.com.
  • 2001 to 2007. Messing About in Boats: booklet advertisements, Glen Maxwell's sea trials of Zoë and the Cedar Key gatherings. Duckworks Magazine reprints Maxwell's sea trials and Derek Clark's beginner's build account.
  • September/October 2003. Small Craft Advisor, Ron Hoddinott's report from the Cedar Key meet, covering Zoë and Whisper and quoting an outside sailor's verdict that "Zoë appeared to be sailing to windward higher and faster than my Sea Pearl with its leeboard down." Not held; see below.
  • June 2006. Sea People no. 64, the Polynesian Catamaran Association's newsletter, with a photograph of Paradox leaving a Wharram catamaran at the Hobe Sound meet of December 2005.
  • 2006 and 2007. Furled Sails, two interviews with the Bolducs (July 2006) and two with Yrvind (July 2007).
  • May 2007. Small Craft Advisor, a first, partial piece on the chine runner, held only as a partial scan.
  • About 2008. Dinghy Cruising Association Bulletin 193: Alastair Law's "Sailing the Paradox", fifteen advantages and eleven disadvantages after three seasons in Little Jim, printed beside Duncan Gilchrist's "Paradox", the sceptic's sail trial at Chew Valley Lake.
  • October 2008. Catalyst no. 32, the journal of the Amateur Yacht Research Society: Sven Yrvind's account of sailing Paradox and of the boat Layden drew for him, with ten Layden drawings.
  • May/June 2009. Small Craft Advisor no. 57: Layden's fullest interview, printed with Doug Cameron's profile of him.
  • July 2009. Messing About in Boats, Bill Serjeant's Dinghy Cruising Association account of Faith's cruise, with Faith on the July 2009 cover, the design's second magazine cover after 1995.
  • 2011 to 2013. Messing About in Boats, the Layden lug and the chine runner in other designers' boats.
  • 2014. SAIL, David Bolduc's "Go Small, Go Now", the one dated end-to-end account of how the Bolducs found Layden.
  • 2014 to 2017. Messing About in Boats, modern coverage.
  • April/May 2021. The Amateur Boat Builders' Association newsletter, Western Australia: Roselt Croeser's junk-rigged Planktos.
  • 9 December 2022. Small Craft Advisor online, the second Layden interview.
  • 2023 to 2025. The first edition of this book, and its audiobook.

Swamp Thing is photographed in Ida Little and Michael Walsh's Beachcruising and Coastal Camping, and Layden's drawings appear in Yrvind's Swedish book KONSTRUKTOREN. Classic Boat of August 2012, long thought to carry Paradox content, was checked page by page and does not.

How the claims were checked

Every chapter of this book began as a research dossier, written against the wiki and the raw sources together, with each finding marked by how much the book needed it and each quotation copied from its source. The dossiers were then reviewed, chapter by chapter, by a second reader working against the raw sources, and the synopsis was rewritten from the review. The chapters were drafted from the dossiers and the raw sources, cited sentence by sentence, and then audited one by one against their sources before being read in sequence as a book.

Four rules governed what the chapters could say.

Source priority. Where sources conflict, Layden's own plans and words come first, then Don Elliott's "Building Paradox", then the magazine pieces, then the builders' firsthand logs, then forum talk. A firsthand account beats a second-hand one. A figure quoted on the list beats a figure remembered years later on Facebook.

Layden verbatim. His words are the one thing the book must get exactly right. Every quotation attributed to him was copied from the raw source, not from the wiki, and the full texts are in appendix D. Everyone else is quoted sparingly, and a paraphrase never stands in quotation marks.

Floors, not estimates. The fleet register in appendix A counts what the record shows and says how sure it is. The book says "at least 69 hulls begun" and "at least 29 confirmed sailing" because those are the hulls the archive can name, and it says plainly that hundreds of plans were sold and that nobody knows how many boats they became.

Honest limits beside every merit. Every chapter states what owners actually report going wrong, with the boat's name and the year, next to what she does well. The plainest limit is stated in the introduction and again where the evidence ends: no Paradox has a confirmed ocean crossing.

What the checking found. The first edition of this book was written with the help of a language model trained on the mailing list, and several of its specifics turned out to have no source at all: a Bolger design called Lion's Paw as the chine runner's ancestor, a "Team Melaleuca" in the 2003 race, a model test in a brook, Terrapin's dimensions and displacement, a fleet of "50 to 100" boats, Elusion's sail area. None of them returns here. The wiki, careful as it is, was found wrong in a handful of places while the chapters were being cited against the raw record: the 2003 Everglades Challenge time on one page (3 days 9 hours 51 minutes, not 10 hours 51), the date of the 2009 interview on the chine-runner pages, a line about Wharram catamarans that reversed its source, a quotation attributed to Bill Serjeant that was Stephen Yahn's, a capsize of Ironsides at Lake Amistad that never happened, the attribution of Layden's list of influences to 2005 rather than 2022, a fleet table that had lost one hull and the time Sean Mulligan took to finish Scout. Each is corrected in the chapter concerned and recorded in the draft's working files.

Privacy

The book will be read by the people in it. The mailing list and the Facebook groups are communities their members joined to talk among themselves, and the book quotes both sparingly and prefers to state facts. Private matters that a builder published on their own blog are used where they explain a boat's story, and private matters mentioned in passing by others are not. No one's legal name is inferred from an email handle; where only a handle is known, the book says the builder gave no name. Deaths are given by year alone. The quarrels and the administration of the list, which every long-running list accumulates, stay out, as do two incidents of malicious software that passed through it. Matt Layden's wife appears by her first name only, his home as Florida's east coast, and nothing is said of his health or his family beyond what he has published himself. He has no children; a line in one auto-generated transcript that says otherwise is a transcription error and has been left out.

What is not held

A few published sources known to exist could not be obtained and are flagged where a chapter would have used them: Small Craft Advisor's report from the 2003 Cedar Key meet (September/October 2003) and its May 2007 chine-runner piece, both available from the magazine's back-issue service; the Amateur Boat Builders' Association newsletters of 2014 and 2015 on Roselt Croeser's earlier boats; Sven Yrvind's 2025 memoir in English; two Sailing Anarchy threads on Yrvind and Paradox; the 2024 Race to Alaska podcast episode with Jolyn Barley; and George Van Sickle's Enigma 460 build blog. The plans themselves were not measured for this draft.
